Greenhouse irrigation timers are usually programmed once at installation and rarely revisited as crops mature or weather changes. Growers at the Ashford Ridge cooperative had also never compared their schedules against runoff records, which made the trial possible. The principal finding is that moving the morning irrigation start from 5:00 to 6:30 reduced substrate runoff by 23 percent across the fourteen bays monitored during the eight-week trial. The evidence has a clear limit: readings came from one growing season, and the moisture loggers were recalibrated only twice, so sensor drift cannot be excluded as a contributor. The scope is also narrow, since the results apply only to tomato and cucumber bays under passive ventilation and say nothing about hydroponic or heated structures. Before any grower changes a timer based on this note, they should confirm that their substrate moisture sensors agree within two percent of a gravimetric check taken on the same day.
