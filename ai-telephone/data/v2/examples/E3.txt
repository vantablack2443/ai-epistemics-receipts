Internal technical note, Brightwater Transport Analytics, file ref. BTA-RN-0417

Background: The Eastfield district adopted the Larkspur routing engine in 2024 after a pilot that the board later judged too brief to settle questions about winter operations. Drivers were not consulted during the original design, and their informal adjustments to stop order were never recorded, which is part of why the current comparison is harder to read than it looks.

Over the autumn term, the optimiser cut total daily mileage across the fourteen Eastfield routes by 11.2 percent, from 1,846 to 1,639 miles, while keeping every student's pickup within the 0.8-mile walking limit. The reduction was largest on the two northern loops, where overlapping stops had previously been served twice. That is the principal finding, and it holds for the weekday schedule as modelled.

The first qualification is that the evidence comes from simulated trips built on enrolment records from September. Actual traffic delays, road closures, and student absences were not measured, so the mileage figure is a model output rather than a fleet log. The second is that the study covers only the Eastfield district and only the morning and afternoon runs; the Coldharbour and Millbrook depots, and any late or activity buses, fall outside it. The third is a condition: before any route is changed, the transport office must confirm with the safeguarding lead that no student's new stop lies outside the agreed supervised-catchment boundaries, because the optimiser does not check this constraint.
