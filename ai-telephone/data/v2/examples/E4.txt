The Vellin bakery oven uses a controller that adjusts heater output in response to a probe near the center of the chamber. Its operator panel retains the previous shift’s settings, and a small indicator lamp changes color when the heating cycle begins.

In a controlled trial, the controller reduced the average difference between the selected temperature and the probe reading by 12 degrees. The improvement appeared after the control software was adjusted to respond more gradually as the chamber approached its setpoint. The resulting temperature trace showed fewer broad swings during the observed heating period, and the controller maintained a steadier reading after the chamber settled. The available evidence comes from a single trial, so it does not establish how consistently the improvement would recur. The finding applies only to temperature control at the probe location and does not establish baking quality elsewhere in the chamber. Before adopting the adjustment, the bakery must verify that the probe is calibrated against a trusted reference. The trial record and the revised settings have been retained for the next review.
