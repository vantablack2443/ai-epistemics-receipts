Bakery Oven Temperature Controller: Field Trial Note

Ovens in small bakeries often drift outside their set points during the first hour of a shift, and the proportional-integral controller on the Halvard K-40 deck oven was the focus of a six-week trial at the Brindle Lane bakery in Wexcombe. The principal finding is that replacing the stock controller's fixed integral gain with a gain scheduled by door-open events reduced peak temperature overshoot from 14 degrees Celsius to 6 degrees across 212 recorded loading cycles. Thermocouple readings were logged at one-second intervals, but the trial ran on a single oven, so the observed effect may reflect that unit's insulation and burner age rather than the design itself. The results apply only to deck ovens running between 200 and 260 degrees Celsius, and they say nothing about convection or rotary models, which heat by different mechanisms. Before any bakery adopts the scheduled-gain firmware, its technician must confirm that the door sensor is wired to the controller's interrupt input, because a floating input produces spurious gain changes. The trial team, led by engineer Odile Varga, also noted that the controller's default firmware was never altered in the baseline run, which is the comparison the finding depends on.
