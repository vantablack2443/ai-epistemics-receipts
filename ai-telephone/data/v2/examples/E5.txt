At the fictional Veylan Museum, conservators placed wireless humidity sensors inside display cases and in the surrounding galleries. The monitoring system recorded readings continuously and compared them with the settings used by the building’s climate controls. Sensor housings were painted to blend with the case interiors. Staff also kept a paper log of battery replacements.

The principal finding was that relative humidity inside the main textile case varied by 4 percentage points over the observation period. The evidence is limited by the absence of an independent calibration check during that period. The finding applies only to the monitored case and cannot establish conditions elsewhere in the museum. Before staff change the climate-control settings, they must verify the sensor reading against a calibrated reference instrument.

The case’s buffered interior may help explain its apparent stability, although the monitoring record alone does not establish why the readings changed. Conservators can retain the record as a baseline for comparison with later observations. The paper log can also help them distinguish a gap in transmission from an interruption in monitoring.
